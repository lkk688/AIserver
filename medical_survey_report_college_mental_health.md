# Medical Survey Report: Mental Health Problems Among Recent College Students

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive medical survey report synthesizes findings from multiple peer-reviewed studies examining mental health problems among college students. The research reveals a significant mental health crisis affecting recent college students, with elevated rates of depression, anxiety, and stress, particularly exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Key findings indicate that approximately one in four undergraduate students screen positive for common mental disorders, with international students experiencing nearly doubled prevalence rates from 2015-2024.

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background
Mental health problems are the first cause of disability worldwide and represent a major public health issue. Depression, anxiety, and stress are considered critical indicators of mental health that, if untreated, can have severe negative effects on individuals. College students are particularly vulnerable to experiencing psychological distress due to academic pressure, financial burden, relationship concerns, and biological factors.

### 1.2 Study Objectives
This report aims to:
- Document the prevalence of depression, anxiety, and stress among college students
- Identify associated risk factors and demographic vulnerabilities
- Examine the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on student mental health
- Analyze mental health service utilization patterns
- Provide evidence-based recommendations for interventions

## 4. Vulnerable Populations

### 4.1 High-Risk Groups

1. **Female Students**: Higher prevalence of depression and anxiety
2. **International Students**: Nearly doubled prevalence rates from 2015-2024
3. **Rural Students**: More vulnerable to mental health issues
4. **Low-Income Students**: Financial stress significantly increases depression, anxiety, and suicidal thoughts
5. **Academically Underperforming Students**: Higher levels of stress, anxiety, and depression
6. **First-Year Students**: May experience higher anxiety than upper-class students
7. **Students Without Stable Partners**: Significantly associated with depressive symptoms

### 4.2 Service Utilization Gaps

Despite high prevalence rates, mental health service utilization remains critically low:

- **60-64%** of students with moderate or severe symptoms never used mental health services
- **57%** of students with positive anxiety/depression screens have not used counseling
- **64%** have not taken psychotropic medications in the past 12 months
- **58.2%** never tried mental health care during COVID-19

## 6. Clinical Assessment Tools

### 6.1 Depression, Anxiety, and Stress Scale (DASS-21)

- **Depression**: >27 = severe; 20-27 = moderate; 13-20 = mild
- **Anxiety**: >19 = severe; 14-19 = moderate; 8-14 = mild
- **Stress**: >33 = severe; 25-33 = moderate; 18-25 = mild

### 6.2 Other Assessment Instruments

- **Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)**: 0-7 = no insomnia; 8-14 = sub-threshold; 15-21 = moderate; 22-28 = severe
- **Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale (RSES)**: ≥30 = high; 26-29 = medium; ≤25 = low
- **Internet Addiction Test (IAT)**: <50 = controlled; 50-79 = excessive; ≥80 = severe

## 7. Recommendations

### 7.1 Institutional Interventions

1. **Proactive Outreach**: Identify students at risk before they seek help
2. **Accessible Care**: Increase availability of mental health services
3. **Culturally Competent Services**: Especially for international and diverse student populations
4. **Early Screening**: Implement routine mental health assessments
5. **Extended Hours**: Provide services during evenings and weekends
6. **Telehealth Options**: Increase remote counseling availability

### 7.2 Prevention Strategies

1. **Promote Mental Health Literacy**: Educate students about recognizing symptoms
2. **Build Psychological Resilience**: Implement wellness programs
3. **Reduce Stigma**: Create supportive campus culture
4. **Academic Support**: Provide resources for academic stress management
5. **Financial Aid**: Address financial stress through scholarships and aid
6. **Sleep Hygiene Programs**: Address insomnia and sleep disruption

### 7.3 Individual-Level Interventions

1. **Encourage Help-Seeking Behavior**: Normalize mental health care
2. **Peer Support Programs**: Establish student-led support networks
3. **Mindfulness and Stress Reduction**: Offer workshops and resources
4. **Physical Activity Promotion**: Encourage regular exercise
5. **Healthy Lifestyle Habits**: Nutrition, sleep, and substance use education

---

## 8. Conclusion

The mental health crisis among recent college students represents a significant public health challenge requiring immediate and sustained attention. With prevalence rates of depression, anxiety, and stress reaching alarming levels—particularly among vulnerable populations like international students, females, and low-income students—universities and healthcare providers must implement comprehensive, proactive interventions.

The gap between need and service utilization (60-64% of affected students never seeking help) indicates that traditional reactive models are insufficient. A shift toward proactive identification, destigmatization, and accessible, culturally competent care is essential.

### Key Takeaways:
- **One in four** undergraduate students screen positive for common mental disorders
- **International students** experienced nearly doubled prevalence rates from 2015-2024
- **COVID-19 pandemic** significantly exacerbated existing mental health problems
- **Service utilization remains critically low** despite high prevalence
- **Comprehensive, multi-level interventions** are urgently needed
